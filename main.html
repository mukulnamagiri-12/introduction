Field notes from Hyderabad

Hyderabad, in motion

Personal essay 8 min read
01

Landing in the bustle

Hyderabad met me the way it meets most people: loud, glittering, and entirely unbothered by whether I was ready for it. I felt lucky just to land a role at a company my father had spoken of with such pride for years — it's hard to forget where you come from once you find yourself standing somewhere better than you ever imagined.

What struck me first wasn't the skyline, though the high-rises were impossible to ignore. It was the current running underneath them. In the engineers I worked alongside, I could see hope moving quietly through everything — a shared appetite to build something new, in a city dense enough to hold the population of a small country and somehow still make room for everyone's ambition.

02

The friendships crept up on me — easy conversations with colleagues, the kind of humor where everyone's fair game, and tea breaks that stretched a little longer than they should have. Someone always had a story about how impossibly cramped their hostel was, told with more affection than complaint. There's a particular comfort in being surrounded by people who are truly in it with you.

Then there were the debugging marathons before a deploy, and the long stretches where nothing seemed to work for weeks at a time. And yet, even in the frustration, there was an unspoken bond in knowing someone else was awake at 2am fighting the same kind of fire. Nobody really knows everything, and somehow everybody knows something — that's the strange, comforting truth of working in tech.

An inferiority complex in the first few weeks, a superiority complex a few weeks after that.

Every new project made a beginner of me again, and I got to watch that cycle repeat itself in everyone around me too. I'm glad to say I came out of it with a few gloriously geeky, genuinely good friends.

03

A kinder kind of ambition

I'd heard about P.N.C. Menon's philanthropic work long before I understood the ecosystem behind it — mostly through my father, who spoke of it with quiet admiration. Being able to work somewhere driven by that same generosity turned out to be one of the more grounding parts of the experience.

The office itself surprised me too — photography lined the walls in a way that felt unexpectedly moving rather than decorative. There's something satisfying about showing up every day to a place shaped by a mission bigger than deadlines. And I never quite stopped being curious about the buildings themselves: how towers like that come together at all. One day, I'd love to sit down with an interior designer and just ask how the magic actually happens.

04

Bricks become homes

Rich or poor, almost everyone shares the same quiet dream: a place to call their own. Some are lucky enough to actually find it — somewhere to put their feet up, share a meal with family, and let an entire childhood unfold into adulthood.

We're strange, sentimental creatures. A home is just brick and cement if you strip it down, and yet it becomes the place where memories are made, children are raised, and whole lives are lived. It's why the work of building homes always felt like more than construction to me.

05

Giving back

What stayed with me most was learning how deliberate the generosity around the office really was. Spirit ran through a dedicated organization, the Sri Kurumba Educational and Charitable Trust, which P.N.C. Menon founded in 1994 to work on rural poverty, education, healthcare, and housing across Southern India.

Where the pledge has gone

Since 2007

Sobha Academy

A school built to give children from low-income, rural families in Kerala genuine access to quality education — the same kind of chance that, elsewhere, gets treated as a given rather than a gift.

Sobha Hermitage

The Community Home Project

The first phase handed over 100 free houses to women-led families in Palakkad district — part of a commitment to build 1,000 homes over four years, each one signed over debt-free.

Sobha Icon

A talent-development track for students from schools with limited resources, pairing them with regular mentoring and progress tracking rather than a one-time scholarship. Four graduates went on to win the Kerala Chief Minister's Student Talent Award.

Young Mother Rehabilitation Programme

Housing, employment, and schooling for young widows and their children in self-contained flats, at no cost. Several of the women it has supported have gone on to remarry with the trust's help.

Family support

In 2016, P.N.C. and Sobha Menon signed the Giving Pledge, committing half their wealth to causes like these. The reach goes beyond Kerala too — a pledge toward the Sabarmati Riverfront development in Ahmedabad, and a contribution to a cancer hospital in Dubai through the Al Jalila Foundation. None of it was advertised on the walls I walked past every day — I only pieced it together later, which somehow made it feel more genuine, not less.

Some cities pass through you. This one, it turns out, stayed.

Still grateful for the chaos and the tea breaks.
